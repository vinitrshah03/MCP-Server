Monkeys are divided into two groups: Old World monkeys (Africa and Asia) and New World monkeys (Central and South America).
Capuchin monkeys are one of the smartest monkey species and can use tools like rocks to crack open nuts.
Spider monkeys have prehensile tails that act like a fifth limb, allowing them to hang from branches and grip objects.
The pygmy marmoset is the world's smallest monkey, weighing only about 100 grams (3.5 ounces) when fully grown.
Howler monkeys are among the loudest land animals - their calls can be heard up to 5 kilometers (3 miles) away through dense forest.
Japanese macaques (snow monkeys) are known for bathing in hot springs during winter to stay warm in snowy climates.
Monkeys are omnivores and eat a variety of foods including fruits, leaves, insects, flowers, and sometimes small animals.
Mandrill monkeys have the most colorful faces of any mammal, with bright blue and red markings that become more vivid in dominant males.
Monkeys live in social groups called troops, which can range from just a few individuals to over 100 members depending on the species.
Unlike apes, monkeys have tails - this is one of the easiest ways to distinguish between monkeys and their tailless ape cousins.
